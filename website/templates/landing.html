What you see while you play

One narrow window beside Arena.

You hear the advice first. The sidebar is the written copy, plus the reasoning behind it.

  1. Turn strip

    Whose turn it is, the turn number and the phase.

  2. Model and source

    The model answering (GLM 5.3 on our server) and where the game state comes from: Log watcher for Arena's own log, Bridge when the autoplay bridge is connected too.

  3. Now

    The line it just said out loud, with the time it said it.

  4. Best line

    A search over your next three turns and their attacks in between. “Board even” is a weighted board score, not a win chance.

  5. Game plan

    Written by the model in the background and checked against board math: outlook (a 0–100 read on the game), role (aggressor, racer or defender), both clocks, the searched line and its alternative, the next three turns and how you win.

  6. Board

    Life totals, your hand with mana costs, both sides' creatures with power, toughness and tapped state, and your lands.

  7. Feed

    Everything it has said this game, newest at the bottom.

  8. Controls

    Autoplay, auto-queue, stop talking, and Voice & style: voice, speed, quick or chatty advice, mute.

What it does

Coaching, drafting, and a pair of hands.

Table talk, out loud

It reads the real game from Arena's log — your hand, both boards, the stack, every legal action — and says the play, so your eyes stay on the game. When their removal targets something you can protect, it tells you to respond before the window closes.

  • 28 English voices
  • Adjustable speed
  • Quick or Chatty
  • Speech runs on your computer
Surrak and Goreclaw is already down — move to combat and swing with everything for lethal. 0:05 · a real line from a coaching log

Drafts for the deck, not the tier list

Every pick comes with its reasons: 17Lands numbers, your lane, the open signals, and how the card's rules text works with what you've taken. When the draft ends you hear a counted 40-card build with every cut named. Sealed pools get a spoken build recommendation too.

On a Mac with the bridge, Autoplay can run the whole event: it researches the set before pick one, drafts for the deck it's building (17Lands is about a quarter of each pick; hate-picks are their own call), submits the 40 and plays the matches.

An autopilot that actually plays

Turn on Autoplay and it plays the match — lands, spells, targets, attacks and blocks — submitted straight to Arena's game engine through the bridge, not by driving your mouse.

  • Plays out whole lines — your next three turns against their worst-case attacks — before it commits to one.
  • Counts your planeswalkers when it blocks and when it weighs the crackback, and weighs loyalty costs against the burn they could be holding.
  • Sticks to its own plan: when the plan says hold back, it holds back. Only an all-in or lethal swing overrides it.
  • Auto-queue starts the next match with the same queue and deck. It concedes only when the board math proves the game is lost, after a countdown you can cancel.
  • No bridge? It says MANUAL REQUIRED, hands you the decision, and keeps coaching.

Knows your deck before the first draw

Open Decks before you queue and it writes a playbook for any deck Arena has shown it: how it wins, what to do when that's disrupted, its engines and key cards. The plan card works from it on turn one.

  1. Before — Tools → Decks builds the playbook.
  2. During — the game plan follows it, turn by turn.
  3. After — Match History logs the result and a 1–10 score the coach gives its own advice.

Quick enough for Arena's clock

Board math happens on your computer; only the judgment calls go to the model. Key decisions ask twice at once — a quick answer and a deeper one — and use the deeper one only if it's back in time.

  1. Log line — read the moment Arena writes it.
  2. Board math — combat, clocks and the three-turn search, in milliseconds.
  3. Model — quick and deep answers race; 15-second ceiling. Quick decisions came back in a median 2.5 s in a live session on 18 Oct 2026.
  4. Voice — spoken on your computer.

Questions

The honest answers.

What does it read?

Arena's own Player.log, with Detailed Logs (Plugin Support) turned on. That is everything the coach knows about your game, so coaching needs nothing installed into Arena. Setup & Repair checks the setting for you.

What does autoplay need?

The bridge: a plugin that runs inside the Arena client and submits actions to its game engine — BepInEx on Windows and under Proton, an injected library on macOS. It is optional. Without it, autoplay says MANUAL REQUIRED, hands the decision back to you, and the coach keeps advising.

Where does the AI run?

On our own GPU server, reached through api.mtgacoach.com; the model is GLM 5.3. There's no per-token billing — your subscription is the whole bill — with fair-use rate limits far above what a game needs. The voice is synthesized on your computer.

What leaves my computer?

Each request sends the game facts the model needs to our server. By default the app also uploads a redacted record of each finished match — its decisions, the coach's log, the match's game events and any bug reports — so we can improve the coach from real games. Names, keys, emails, file paths and Arena account IDs are removed first.

Turn it off in Tools → Share Match Logs to Improve the Coach.

Does my subscription cover ArenaOnAir?

Yes. Paste the same Patreon key into ArenaOnAir's Premium connection. Its five free matches are separate from mtgacoach's 7-day trial.

How do I cancel?

Cancel your pledge on Patreon. Your key stops working when the pledge ends; nothing is billed here.

Is this official?

No. mtgacoach is unofficial fan software, not made or endorsed by Wizards of the Coast. Magic: The Gathering is a trademark of Wizards of the Coast LLC.
